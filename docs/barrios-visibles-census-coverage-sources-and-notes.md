# Barrios Visibles: census coverage, imputation, and comparative precedents

**Research notes from the September 2026 discussion**  
**Purpose:** handoff for integration into the Barrios Visibles Zotero/GitHub/Quarto literature system.  
**Status:** working notes, not publication-ready prose.

## Executive synthesis

The discussion established five distinct propositions:

1. A plausible national population total does not prove that a census directly enumerated everyone.
2. Published census totals can include large numbers of imputed residents.
3. Post-enumeration surveys can detect large gross omissions even after imputation.
4. National demographic reconciliation does not repair missing or misallocated people in small-area census geography.
5. A roughly three-million-person discrepancy in Argentina is surprising but not inherently implausible. Recent Latin American censuses have reported errors of the same proportional magnitude or larger.

The strongest regional comparison is Brazil's 2022 Census. Brazil directly enumerated about 195.1 million people, raised the published Census population to 203.1 million through imputation for occupied non-interviewed households, subsequently measured 12.2% person omission and 8.3% net coverage error through a post-enumeration survey, and produced a revised 2022 demographic estimate of 210.9 million. The revised projection incorporated the Census, the post-enumeration survey, and demographic reconciliation. It did **not** rewrite the published small-area Census tables.

This is important for Barrios Visibles because imputation can only operate on dwellings or households the census system identifies. It cannot reliably recover hidden subdivisions, rear dwellings, upper-floor households, or buildings omitted from the census frame. Those are the same mechanisms documented in Argentine informal settlements and detected indirectly by the building-footprint analysis.

## Claim boundaries

### Strongly supported

- Errors on the scale proposed by Barrios Visibles occur in modern Latin American censuses.
- Gross omission, net coverage error, imputation, and demographic reconciliation are different quantities.
- A close national demographic total can coexist with millions of actual enumeration omissions.
- Dense informal settlements and multi-dwelling plots create known enumeration problems.
- Argentina's published 2022 small-area data contain at least one officially documented, extremely large omission in an informal settlement.
- The Barrios Visibles method is conservative in dense, vertically developed settlements because it retains higher INDEC estimates and does not infer additional households within a footprint.

### Supported by Barrios Visibles analysis, but not proved by comparative literature alone

- Roughly three million residents are missing from the published census geography of Argentina's informal settlements.
- Nearby formal census tracts do not contain enough excess population to explain the discrepancy through simple local misassignment.

### Remaining uncertainty

- Public data cannot determine how much of the estimated informal-settlement shortfall is absent from Argentina's national total versus counted through imputation, duplication, erroneous inclusion, or assignment elsewhere.
- Comparative evidence makes a national omission of this magnitude plausible; it does not independently prove Argentina's exact total.

## Core concepts

### Direct enumeration

People actually recorded from household responses or field enumeration.

### Imputation

Statistical assignment of people or characteristics where a dwelling is known or inferred but a complete interview was not obtained. Imputation improves completeness but is not equivalent to directly reaching residents. It is weakest where the underlying dwelling or household was never identified.

### Gross omission

People who should have been included but were not correctly enumerated. This can be large even when the final national total is close to an independent population estimate.

### Erroneous inclusion

Duplicated people, people counted in the wrong place or at the wrong residence, and other records that should not appear in the count.

### Net coverage error

The balance after omissions and erroneous inclusions offset one another. A small net error does not imply a small number of omissions.

### Demographic reconciliation or population projection

A calculation using prior population stocks, births, deaths, migration, census results, and sometimes coverage-study evidence to estimate a plausible population total. It evaluates aggregate demographic consistency. It does not show that each person was enumerated or correctly located.

## Brazil: the critical precedent

### IBGE, *Pesquisa de Pós-Enumeração do Censo Demográfico 2022*

- **Source:** [IBGE post-enumeration survey landing page](https://www.ibge.gov.br/estatisticas/sociais/saude/40418-pesquisa-de-pos-enumeracao-do-censo-demografico-2022.html)
- **Type:** primary official post-enumeration survey.
- **Key findings discussed:** 12.2% person omission; 3.3% erroneous inclusion; 8.3% net coverage error.
- **Why it matters:** This is direct coverage measurement, not merely a disagreement with projections. The proportional omission is substantially larger than a three-million-person discrepancy against Argentina's 2022 population.
- **Mechanism:** IBGE materials and reporting identify access problems in favelas and dense communities, including multiple dwellings on a single plot where only the front dwelling is discovered.
- **Use in article:** strongest answer to the claim that a roughly 6% Argentine discrepancy is intrinsically impossible.
- **Check before quotation:** retrieve the final technical tables and exact definitions of the denominators used for gross omission and net error.

### Brazil's enumerated, imputed, and projected populations

- **Published final Census population:** 203,080,756. See [IBGE's 2022 Census summary](https://educa.ibge.gov.br/jovens/conheca-o-brasil/populacao/22005-censo-2022-o-retrato-atualizado-do-brasil.html).
- **Directly enumerated population:** approximately 195.1 million.
- **Difference attributable to population imputation in occupied households without completed interviews:** approximately 8.0 million.
- **Revised estimate for 1 July 2022:** 210,862,983.
- **Difference from the final Census population:** 7,782,227, or about 3.8%.
- **Projection source:** [IBGE population projections, 2024 revision](https://www.ibge.gov.br/estatisticas/sociais/populacao/9109-projecao-da-populacao.html).
- **Method source:** [IBGE presentation on the 2024 projection revision](https://agenciadenoticias.ibge.gov.br/media/com_mediaibge/arquivos/9c05a15727b2454a8640369bf9c93c57.pdf). The presentation states that the revision includes the 2022 Census, post-enumeration survey data, and demographic reconciliation for the 27 states.
- **Accessible secondary summary:** [Agência Brasil, "IBGE estima percentual de inconsistências dos dados do Censo 2022"](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/geral/noticia/2024-08/ibge-estima-percentual-de-inconsistencias-dos-dados-do-censo-2022).

#### Interpretation

Brazil presents three different, legitimate population quantities:

1. people directly enumerated;
2. the published Census population after imputation;
3. a later demographic estimate incorporating the Census, coverage measurement, and demographic accounting.

These quantities answer different questions. The published Census tables remained at the Census count; the revised projection changed the demographic baseline. Consequently, the later national estimate does not repair the original small-area geography.

#### Important correction from the discussion

An intermediate answer incorrectly suggested that the 210.9 million projection did not incorporate the post-enumeration survey. IBGE's own methodology presentation says that it **did** incorporate post-enumeration survey data, alongside Census data and demographic reconciliation. The accurate formulation is:

> Brazil did not simply replace its Census tables with the post-enumeration estimate. It used the Census, post-enumeration evidence, and demographic reconciliation to produce a revised population projection while leaving the published Census geography intact.

## Argentina: direct and journalistic evidence

### IDECBA: Barrio Padre Ricciardelli / Villa 1-11-14

- **Source:** IDECBA, [*Los Barrios Populares Informales (BaPIs) de la Ciudad de Buenos Aires, 2001–2022, parte 1*](https://www.estadisticaciudad.gob.ar/eyc/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Los_barrios_populares_BaPls_2002_2022_parte_01.pdf).
- **Type:** primary official statistical report from the City of Buenos Aires.
- **Relevant section:** Annex II, evaluation of 2022 Census results for Barrio Padre Ricciardelli, starting around printed page 29.
- **Comparison:** INDEC's F2 *Resumen de Fracción* operational sheets versus INDEC's published Redatam results for the same radios.
- **Operational sheets:** 4,906 visited dwellings and 23,213 people.
- **Published Redatam data:** 1,118 dwellings and 3,640 people.
- **Difference:** 3,788 dwellings and 19,573 people.
- **IDECBA's description:** "subenumeración u omisión censal."
- **Why it matters:** This is nearly direct evidence that people present in INDEC's own field-summary records disappeared between field collection and the published small-area data. It does not rely on building footprints, occupancy assumptions, or Barrios Visibles.
- **Geographic caution:** The figures above concern the villa-sector radios analyzed in the annex, not necessarily every housing complex included in broader definitions of Barrio Padre Ricciardelli.

### Fundación Tejido Urbano: independent detection of the same anomaly

- **Source:** [*Radiografía del crecimiento de villas y barrios populares de la Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires, 2010–2022*](https://tejidourbano.org.ar/informes/radiografia-del-crecimiento-de-villas-y-barrios-populares-de-la-ciudad-autonoma-de-buenos-aires-2010-2022/).
- **Follow-up housing report:** [*Síntesis de la radiografía de la situación habitacional en las villas y barrios populares de la CABA*](https://tejidourbano.org.ar/informes/sintesis-de-la-radiografia-de-la-situacion-habitacional-en-las-villas-barrios-populares-de-la-caba/).
- **Type:** independent urban-policy research using published Census data.
- **Finding discussed:** the published population associated with Villa 1-11-14 fell from 28,259 in 2010 to 6,868 in 2022, an apparent decline of 21,391 people or roughly 76%.
- **Procedure:** Tejido Urbano checked the decline radio by radio, found it throughout the settlement, judged the 2022 values extremely low and unreliable, and excluded/substituted the result in later analysis.
- **Why it matters:** Tejido Urbano detected the anomaly from final tract data; IDECBA later traced a closely related shortfall through operational field sheets. The geographic units and totals are not identical, but the independent analyses point to the same failure in the same barrio.

### LA NACION: enumeration conditions in Villa 21-24

- **Source:** ["Golpeás una puerta y no sabés cuántas familias vas a encontrar: el complejo operativo para relevar una villa porteña"](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/golpeas-una-puerta-y-no-sabes-cuantas-familias-vas-a-encontrar-el-complejo-operativo-para-relevar-nid18052022/).
- **Type:** contemporaneous field reporting on Census Day.
- **Findings discussed:** buildings up to five stories; narrow internal passages; several households behind a single apparent entrance; an *inquilinato* containing as many as ten households; enumerators reporting difficulty tracking which dwellings had been visited.
- **Why it matters:** documents the mechanism by which both a conventional census and a one-footprint/one-household model can miss residents.
- **Effect on Barrios Visibles:** Barrios Visibles does not infer these additional households. Where INDEC reports a higher population than the building-based floor, the analysis retains INDEC's value. Hidden vertical or subdivided households therefore bias the estimate downward.

### LA NACION Data: reported Census omissions

- **Source:** ["Mapa: los reportes de lectores que no fueron censados"](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/mapa-los-reportes-de-lectores-que-no-fueron-censados-nid20052022/).
- **Type:** data journalism and validated reader reports immediately after Census Day.
- **Finding:** LA NACION Data gathered, checked, and mapped reports of households not visited by enumerators.
- **Limit:** establishes real operational omissions nationally; it does not by itself show that omissions were concentrated in informal settlements.

### Clarín: anticipated undercount in informal settlements

- **Source:** [Clarín, report on the 2022 Census operation in villas and settlements](https://www.clarin.com/ciudades/censo-2022-relevara-poblacion-villas-asentamientos-ciudad_0_Z3OMMd3cPq.html).
- **Type:** pre-Census reporting quoting Buenos Aires City statistical officials.
- **Finding discussed:** officials anticipated a likely *subconteo*, particularly in informal neighborhoods, and planned a special operation involving residents and community organizations familiar with internal settlement geography.
- **Why it matters:** undercount risk was anticipated before the results existed; this is not a retrospective explanation invented to fit Barrios Visibles.
- **Follow-up needed:** preserve the exact official quotations and speaker names in Zotero annotations before publication.

### Demographic research on hidden room-rental markets

- **Source:** [peer-reviewed article hosted by AmeliCA](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/80/805238006/html/).
- **Type:** demographic research.
- **Finding discussed:** informal room rentals and households concealed within dense villas can cause household underestimation in census operations.
- **Use:** supports the multiple-households-per-visible-structure mechanism.
- **Follow-up needed:** capture complete bibliographic metadata, exact pages/sections, and a precise quotation in Zotero. Do not cite this note alone.

## Gridded population precedent

### Thomson et al. (2021)

- **Source:** D. R. Thomson et al., ["Evaluating the Accuracy of Gridded Population Estimates in Slums: A Case Study in Nigeria and Kenya"](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/10/6/381).
- **Type:** peer-reviewed comparison of gridded population products with field-referenced settlement populations.
- **Sample:** 118 informal settlements: 26 in Lagos, 39 in Port Harcourt, and 53 in Nairobi.
- **Products:** nine widely used gridded population datasets, including WorldPop, LandScan, HRSL, GRID3, and GHS-POP.
- **Finding discussed:** every product substantially underestimated population in these settlements; the best-performing product captured only about 39% of field-referenced residents.
- **Use:** demonstrates that errors of approximately 60% within informal settlements are empirically documented.
- **Critical limitation:** these are modeled gridded population products, not national census enumeration. The paper establishes precedent for the *magnitude and geographic concentration* of error, not proof that INDEC committed the same error.

## Other census precedents

The cases below are useful for showing that a national discrepancy near 6% falls inside the observed Latin American range. They must be labeled by method.

### Direct post-enumeration or post-census evaluation

#### Peru, 2017

- **Primary source:** INEI, [*Características de la población*](https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/publicaciones_digitales/Est/Lib1539/cap01.pdf).
- **Enumerated:** 29,381,884.
- **Estimated omitted population:** 1,855,501.
- **Adjusted total:** 31,237,385.
- **Omission rate:** 5.94%.
- **Method:** post-enumeration *Encuesta de Evaluación Censal*.
- **Relevance:** nearly identical proportional scale to a three-million-person Argentine discrepancy.

#### Chile, 2012

- **Finding discussed:** an independent expert commission estimated roughly 9.3% omission, approximately 1.6 million people, and recommended against using the Census as the official population baseline.
- **Relevance:** major Latin American operational failure with institutional consequences.
- **Status:** **requires primary-source verification before use.** Locate and archive the commission's report; do not rely on generic Reuters or AP home-page links.

#### South Africa, 2022

- **Primary source:** Statistics South Africa, [2022 Post-enumeration Survey report](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P03015/P030152022.pdf).
- **Finding discussed:** 31.06% net person undercount and 30.49% household undercount.
- **Relevance:** establishes an extreme modern upper bound.
- **Caution:** the methodology and adjusted results have been heavily disputed. Use only as a secondary comparison, paired with the [peer-reviewed critique by Moultrie and Dorrington](https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S0038-23532024000700012&script=sci_arttext).

### Primarily demographic reconciliation or adjustment

#### Colombia, 2018

- **Official materials:** [DANE 2018 Census portal](https://www.dane.gov.co/index.php/estadisticas-por-tema/demografia-y-poblacion/censo-nacional-de-poblacion-y-vivenda-2018) and [DANE demographic-methods material](https://www.dane.gov.co/index.php/estadisticas-por-tema/demografia-y-poblacion/metodologias-demograficas-aplicadas).
- **Accessible contemporary report:** [La República, "Tasa de población omitida en el censo de 2018 fue de 8,5%"](https://www.larepublica.co/economia/tasa-de-poblacion-omitida-en-el-censo-de-2018-fue-de-85-de-acuerdo-con-el-dane-2922475).
- **Finding discussed:** approximately 8.5% omission, roughly four million people.
- **Method caution:** classify this as demographic adjustment/reconciliation unless the specific DANE document establishes an independent dual-system or post-enumeration estimate for the quoted number.

#### Guatemala, 2018

- **Source:** [Prensa Libre account of INE reconciliation](https://www.prensalibre.com/guatemala/comunitario/censo-2018-hay-16-3-millones-de-guatemaltecos/).
- **Finding discussed:** the field census produced about 14.9 million residents; demographic reconciliation added approximately 1.4 million, yielding about 16.3 million, an implied difference near 9%.
- **Method:** demographic reconciliation, not direct post-enumeration measurement.
- **Follow-up:** replace or supplement with the underlying INE technical report.

#### Ecuador, 2022

- **Source:** [INEC statement on the validity and reliability of Census 2022](https://www.ecuadorencifras.gob.ec/el-inec-ratifico-la-validez-veracidad-y-confiabilidad-de-los-resultados-del-censo-ecuador-2022/).
- **Finding discussed:** 4.6% omission incorporated into the adjusted population.
- **Follow-up:** verify the exact method and denominator in the technical report before making a direct comparison.

### Historical Latin American overview

- **Source:** Odette Tacla Chamy, CEPAL/CELADE, [*La omisión censal en América Latina, 1950–2000*](https://repositorio.cepal.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/75308871-335d-4008-b768-2630150b15b5/content).
- **Type:** regional historical methodological review.
- **Use:** documents repeated national omission estimates above 7–15% and emphasizes that national averages conceal much larger geographic differences.
- **Important examples discussed:** Colombia 13.9% in 1970 and 11.3% in 1990; Guatemala 15.6% in 1980 and 14.5% in 1990; Venezuela 8.9% in 1990 and 7.5% in 2001.
- **Caution:** these are principally indirect demographic estimates and historical comparisons, not all direct post-enumeration surveys.

## Gross versus net error: United States example

- **Primary source:** U.S. Census Bureau, [2020 Census coverage estimates](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-kits/2022/2020-census-coverage-estimates.html).
- **Finding:** the post-enumeration survey estimated 18.8 million omissions even though national net coverage error was not statistically significant.
- **Accessible explanation:** Population Reference Bureau, ["How Accurate Was the 2020 Census—and Why Should You Care?"](https://www.prb.org/resources/how-accurate-was-the-2020-census-and-why-should-you-care/), which reports a net undercount estimate of roughly 782,000 after approximately 18 million erroneous enumerations offset the omissions.
- **Use:** clearest demonstration that "the national total is close" and "everyone was counted" are fundamentally different claims.

## Implications for the Barrios Visibles argument

### What the comparative record closes off

The comparative evidence makes the following objections untenable without additional evidence:

- "Three million is too large to be credible."
- "A plausible national total proves that the Census reached these residents."
- "Imputation necessarily fixes omitted households."
- "Large local omissions cannot coexist with a coherent national reconciliation."

### What still requires careful wording

The strongest defensible conclusion is:

> Barrios Visibles provides strong evidence that roughly three million residents are missing from the published census and RENABAP geography of Argentina's informal settlements. Comparative census evidence shows that omissions of this proportional magnitude are entirely credible. Public data do not establish how many are absent from the national total rather than imputed, duplicated, erroneously included, or assigned elsewhere.

The nearby-tract analysis weakens simple local misassignment as a complete explanation, but it cannot trace individual records nationally.

### Why the building method remains conservative

- The conservative scenario removes footprints smaller than 10 m².
- It assumes only 85% of remaining footprints are occupied residences.
- It permits only one household per footprint.
- The method retains INDEC's higher estimate wherever INDEC exceeds the building-based floor.
- It does not recover multiple households in subdivided buildings, rented rooms, upper stories, or *inquilinatos*.

Consequently, documented vertical subdivision and multiple-household occupancy cannot inflate the Barrios Visibles result. They create downward bias.

## Suggested journalistic structure

These notes support a longer investigation rather than a greatly expanded methods blog post:

1. **The discrepancy:** buildings and residents are not where official systems say they should be.
2. **Why the physical evidence is credible:** footprint validation, conservative dwelling yield, temporal checks, and nearby-tract analysis.
3. **Why the magnitude is plausible:** Thomson et al., Brazil, Peru, and other regional cases.
4. **Enumeration is not the published total:** direct responses, imputation, erroneous inclusion, net error, and reconciliation.
5. **Argentina's warning signs:** anticipated undercount, Villa 21-24 field conditions, Tejido Urbano, and IDECBA.
6. **What remains unknown:** national omission versus misallocation or statistical adjustment.
7. **What INDEC and RENABAP need to explain or release.**

The Brazil section can carry the conceptual explanation because it supplies concrete numbers for all three layers: enumeration, imputation, and revised demographic estimation.
