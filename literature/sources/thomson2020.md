Thomson and colleagues argue for an integrated deprived-area mapping
system in low and middle income countries, and explain why four common
approaches stay siloed: census aggregation, community mapping, image
interpretation, and machine learning.

The relevant point for Barrios Visibles is the stated weakness of
census aggregation. It measures household deprivation well and often
lacks current neighbourhood geography, which is the gap that building
footprints fill.
